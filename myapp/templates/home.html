Why Donate Blood?

Every drop matters. Understand the lifesaving impact of a single donation.

🩸

Saves Up to 3 Lives

A single whole blood donation can be separated into red cells, platelets, and plasma — each helping a different patient. One act. Three lives saved.

⏱️

Takes Only 10 Minutes

The actual blood draw lasts about 10 minutes. Including registration and rest time, the full process takes under 45 minutes — less than a coffee break.

💪

Good for Your Health

Regular donation helps reduce iron overload, stimulates new blood cell production, and provides a free mini health screening with every visit.

🌍

Always in Demand

Blood cannot be manufactured. Hospitals need a constant fresh supply for surgeries, accidents, cancer treatment, and childbirth complications — every single day.

🩺 About BloodLink

Connecting Donors.
Saving Lives.

BloodLink is a community-driven blood donor registry built to bridge the gap between people in need and willing donors — quickly, securely, and for free.

We believe no one should lose a loved one because a compatible donor couldn't be found in time. Our platform makes it simple to register as a donor, search by blood group and location, and connect directly within minutes.

Every registered donor has a verified profile. Every request is logged. And your privacy is always protected — contact details are only revealed after a donor approves a request.

Am I Eligible to Donate?

A quick checklist before you enroll as a donor.

✅ You CAN donate if you…

  • Are between 18 and 65 years old
  • Weigh at least 45 kg
  • Are in good general health today
  • Have not donated blood in the last 90 days
  • Have not had a tattoo or piercing in the last 6 months
  • Are not on regular prescription medication

❌ Temporary restrictions apply if you…

  • Have a cold, flu, or active infection
  • Are pregnant or recently gave birth
  • Had a major surgery in the last 6 months
  • Recently travelled to a malaria-prone area
  • Are on antibiotics or blood-thinning medication
  • Have a history of hepatitis B or HIV
